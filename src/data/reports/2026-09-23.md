# Game Industry Health Snapshot

## Industry summary

The figures in this snapshot show an industry in which consumer scale and employment conditions are moving very differently. Newzoo forecasts global games revenue of **$213.9B in 2026**, an increase of **6.1% year over year**, while the global player population is forecast to reach **3.70B**, up **4.4%**. The forecast also counts **1.65B spenders**. BCG's consumer research points in the same direction on engagement: **55%** of surveyed gamers said they had increased their playing time during the previous six months. These are forecasts and survey findings rather than final full-year results, but they describe a large and still-expanding audience.

Direct platform activity adds a current observation to those annual estimates. Steam reported **26.35M concurrent users** at the collection point on 23 September. This figure covers Steam rather than the entire market, and the point-in-time count will vary by collection time, but it establishes the first observation for a possible platform-specific series.

Employment data presents a more constrained picture. DevQuest tracked **6,171 open roles across 315 studios** on 22 September. That headline number is substantial, but the composition of those vacancies matters. Only **366 roles, or 5.9%**, were entry-level, compared with **2,942 mid-level roles**. Engineering accounted for **30.4%** of openings, while **12.2%** were fully remote. Hiring was also concentrated: **49%** of all listed positions sat with only 20 companies. In addition, **1,634 listings, or 26.5%**, had remained open for at least 90 days. The available jobs therefore skew toward experienced candidates, particular disciplines and a relatively small group of large employers.

Recruitment was not static: **451 roles** had opened within the previous seven days. That influx sits beside the large pool of older listings, so the snapshot records both active posting and persistent vacancies. It does not show how many advertised roles were ultimately filled. Future snapshots can distinguish newly listed and removed vacancies and measure listing survival, but this first observation serves only as the baseline for those comparisons.

Layoff tracking shows that workforce contraction continued alongside those vacancies. ASGC recorded **10,140 confirmed layoffs** by 11 August and projected **14,666** for the full year. Xbox separately announced **268 role eliminations** on 22 September. Public-company results were not uniform: Microsoft reported Xbox content and services revenue down **10% year over year**, Ubisoft's Q1 net bookings declined **9.2%**, and Electronic Arts reported FY26 net bookings growth of **9%**. Capcom, Square Enix and Nintendo reported substantially larger increases in their disclosed measures, although those figures cover different periods and business definitions and cannot be treated as a direct ranking.

Product results were similarly uneven. Ubisoft reported **3.5M sold-in copies in 14 days** for Assassin's Creed Black Flag Resynced, while Capcom reported **more than 1M launch-day units** for Onimusha: Way of the Sword. Taken together, the data describes a market with expanding audience scale, meaningful product successes and sharply uneven outcomes for workers and companies.
