# Game Industry Health Snapshot
**Published:** 23 September 2026  
**Reporting period:** 9–23 September 2026

## Industry summary

The figures in this snapshot show an industry in which consumer scale and employment conditions are moving very differently. Newzoo forecasts global games revenue of **$213.9B in 2026**, an increase of **6.1% year over year**, while the global player population is forecast to reach **3.70B**, up **4.4%**. The forecast also counts **1.65B spenders**. BCG's consumer research points in the same direction on engagement: **55%** of surveyed gamers said they had increased their playing time during the previous six months. These are forecasts and survey findings rather than final full-year results, but they describe a large and still-expanding audience.

Employment data presents a more constrained picture. DevQuest tracked **6,171 open roles across 315 studios** on 22 September. That headline number is substantial, but the composition of those vacancies matters. Only **366 roles, or 5.9%**, were entry-level, compared with **2,942 mid-level roles**. Engineering accounted for **1,879 openings**, or **30.4%** of the total, while **753 roles, or 12.2%**, were fully remote. Hiring was also concentrated: **49%** of all listed positions sat with only 20 companies. In addition, **1,634 listings, or 26.5%**, had remained open for at least 90 days. The available jobs therefore skew toward experienced candidates, particular disciplines and a relatively small group of large employers.

Recruitment was not static: **451 roles** had opened within the previous seven days. That influx sits beside the large pool of older listings, so the snapshot records both active posting and persistent vacancies at the same time. It does not show how many advertised roles were ultimately filled, only what studios publicly listed when the data was collected.

Layoff tracking shows that workforce contraction continued alongside those vacancies. ASGC's confirmed 2026 total increased from **9,464 on 10 July to 10,140 on 11 August**. Its full-year forecast rose from **13,878 to 14,666** over the same period. During this report's two-week window, Game Developer carried six stories involving layoffs, closures or studio wind-downs. Xbox separately announced **268 role eliminations** on 22 September, together with further consolidation and a proposed Ninja Theory closure.

Public-company results were not uniform. Microsoft reported Xbox content and services revenue down **10% year over year**, and Ubisoft's Q1 net bookings declined **9.2%**. Electronic Arts reported FY26 net bookings growth of **9%**. Capcom's quarterly net sales increased **54.7%**, while Square Enix reported **51.7%** growth in Digital Entertainment sales. Nintendo's April–June operating profit increased **150.5%**, although the company said the comparison included a material benefit from a US tariff refund. Because these disclosures measure different parts of each business, they show company-level variation rather than a direct ranking.

Recent releases reinforce that variation. Ubisoft reported **3.5M sold-in copies in 14 days** for Assassin's Creed Black Flag Resynced while continuing a restructuring announced before release. Capcom reported more than **1M launch-day units** for Onimusha: Way of the Sword alongside strong quarterly sales and profit growth. Taken together, the data describes a market with expanding consumer reach, meaningful product successes and sharply uneven outcomes for workers and companies. The tables below preserve the individual measures so those differences remain visible.
