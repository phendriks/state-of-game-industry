# Game Industry Health Snapshot

## Industry summary

The enlarged source set gives this snapshot a broader view of the industry without producing one artificial global total. Newzoo forecasts **$213.9B** in 2026 games revenue and **3.70B players**, while Famitsu estimates that the 2025 game-content market reached **JPY32.10T ($214.56B)**. These figures use different definitions, periods and methods, so they reinforce the scale of the market rather than validate one another. Regional observations add useful shape: 16 European markets generated **EUR29.7B** in 2025, Niko Partners estimates **$88.9B** across 13 Asian and Middle Eastern markets, and Japan's market reached an estimated **JPY2.5T**, up **8.0%**. More recent US data was weaker, with July spending of **$4.528B**, down **10%** year over year.

Audience data remains large across several distinct measures. Video Games Europe and EGDF count **199M players** across their 16 covered markets. ESA research estimates **212.3M Americans** aged 5–90 play for at least an hour each week, equal to **67%** of that population. Sony reported a June record of **125M PlayStation monthly active users**, up **2%** year over year. Steam showed **26.35M concurrent users** at the collection point on 23 September. These numbers describe different populations and time windows—people, monthly accounts and a point-in-time platform count—and should not be added together.

Business behaviour suggests that studios are adapting to production and financing risk. In Unity's survey of 300 developers, **52%** said they were prioritising smaller projects to reduce financial and development risk, while **24%** were pursuing revenue beyond primary game sales. Capital remained available in the second quarter: Aream & Co. and InvestGame tracked **$3.1B** in private investment across 108 deals and **$2.3B** in merger-and-acquisition value across 54 transactions. Those deal totals provide evidence of activity, but not an even distribution of funding among studios.

Employment remains the clearest area of strain. DevQuest tracked **6,171 openings across 315 studios** on 22 September, yet only **366**, or **5.9%**, were entry-level. Nearly half of all vacancies sat with the 20 largest hirers, and **26.5%** of listings had remained open for at least 90 days. Against that hiring pool, ASGC had recorded **10,140 confirmed layoffs** by 11 August, with a full-year forecast of **14,666**. The wider European industry employed **123,000 people** in 2025, but this annual stock measure does not negate the current pattern of layoffs and limited junior access.

Company results underline how uneven the market is. Tencent reported **RMB47.3B** in domestic games revenue for the second quarter, up **17%**, while international games revenue was **RMB18.6B**, down **0.8%** as reported but up **4%** at constant currency. NetEase reported **RMB25.0B** from games and related services, compared with RMB22.8B a year earlier. Elsewhere, Xbox content and services and Ubisoft net bookings declined, while Electronic Arts, Capcom, Square Enix and Nintendo reported growth in their respective measures. Because periods, currencies and accounting definitions differ, these are individual company observations rather than a league table.

Overall, the data shows a large audience, continued regional revenue and active capital markets alongside difficult labour conditions and sharply varied company outcomes.
