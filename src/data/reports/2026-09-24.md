# Game Industry Data Snapshot

## Industry summary

The broadest forward-looking numbers remain positive. Newzoo forecasts global games revenue of $213.9 billion for 2026, up 6.1% year over year, alongside 3.70 billion global players, up 4.4%, and 1.65 billion spenders. In a separate forecast for 13 markets across Asia and MENA, Niko Partners puts 2026 revenue at $91.8 billion, up 3.4%, while its 2025 estimate for the same scope was $88.9 billion, up 2.6% year over year. These figures are not directly comparable across scopes, but together they show continued expansion in both audience and monetized activity.

Recent reported spending data in the United States was softer. Circana said July 2026 consumer games spending totaled $4.5 billion, down 10% year over year. Within that total, content accounted for $4.1 billion, hardware $282 million, and accessories $178 million. Hardware was the weakest category, falling 29% from a year earlier, while accessories declined 6%. The reported figures suggest that overall monthly spending stayed large, but hardware demand in particular was under pressure.

Regional 2025 figures also show sizable markets with different mixes. Video Games Europe and EGDF reported European games revenue of €29.7 billion across 16 markets, with 91% digital and 9% physical, and 199 million players, equal to 51% of people aged 6 to 75 in the covered markets. Famitsu’s estimate for the global game-content market in 2025 was JPY32.10 trillion, or $214.56 billion, up 3.5% nominally and 5.0% in constant currency, while Japan’s game-content market was estimated at JPY2.5 trillion, up 8.0% year over year. These reported and estimated figures are tied to different market definitions, but each points to a large, active consumer base.

Other reported indicators suggest a busy but uneven business environment. In Q2 2026, global games private investment reached $3.1 billion across 108 deals, and M&A value reached $2.3 billion across 54 transactions. On the labor side, DevQuest tracked 6,171 open roles across 315 studios, with 47.7% mid-level and 5.9% entry-level; 26.5% of listings had been open for 90+ days and 12.2% were fully remote. Separate tracker data showed 10,140 confirmed layoffs through Aug. 11, with a full-year 2026 projection of 14,666. Taken together, the figures show a market that is still large and growing in aggregate, while spending, hiring and restructuring remain uneven across segments.
