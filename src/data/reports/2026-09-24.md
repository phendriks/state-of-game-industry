# Game Industry Data Snapshot

## Industry summary

The latest global outlook points to continued expansion in both audience and spending. Newzoo’s 2026 forecast puts worldwide games revenue at $213.9 billion, up 6.1% year over year, with the player base projected at 3.70 billion, up 4.4%, and spenders at 1.65 billion, up 4.7%. Those forecasts suggest the market is still broadening as well as deepening, with growth coming from a larger base of people who play and a large cohort that pays. Separately, Niko Partners forecasts $91.8 billion in revenue across 13 markets in Asia and MENA in 2026, up 3.4%, while excluding console revenue in Southeast Asia. This sits alongside its estimate of $88.9 billion for 2025, which was up 2.6% year over year. In Europe, reported 2025 revenue across 16 markets was €29.7 billion, with 91% digital and 9% physical, and the region had 199 million players, equal to 51% of people aged 6 to 75 in those markets.

Reported spending and participation figures show a mixed picture by market and category. In the United States, monthly games spending in July 2026 was $4.5 billion, down 10% year over year. Within that total, content accounted for $4.1 billion, hardware for $282 million and accessories for $178 million. Hardware was the weakest of the three, falling 29% year over year, while accessories declined 6%. On the audience side, 212.3 million people in the US were weekly video-game players in the February 2026 survey, equal to 67% of Americans aged 5 to 90 and up 7.2 million from 2025. In Europe, players were reported at 199 million for 2025, showing a large, established user base even as monetization remained heavily digital.

Company and market-level results were similarly uneven, with some businesses posting strong gains and others showing pressure. Tencent reported Q2 2026 domestic games revenue of RMB47.3 billion, up 17% year over year, while international games revenue was RMB18.6 billion, down 0.8% reported but up 4% at constant currency. NetEase reported RMB25.0 billion in games and related-services revenue for Q2 2026, compared with RMB22.8 billion a year earlier. Capcom reported quarterly net sales of JPY70.41 billion, up 54.7%, and operating profit of JPY41.05 billion, up 66.9%; Square Enix’s Digital Entertainment sales rose 51.7%. Nintendo’s operating profit rose 150.5% in April to June, including a material US tariff refund. On the products side, Assassin’s Creed Black Flag Resynced sold-in copies reached 3.5 million in the first 14 days, Onimusha: Way of the Sword sold more than 1 million units on launch day, and the Onimusha series surpassed 10 million cumulative units. Hiring data also points to a still-active labor market: DevQuest tracked 6,171 openings across 315 studios, with 5.9% entry-level, 12.2% fully remote and 26.5% open for 90 days or more, while Insomniac listed 8 openings and Xbox announced 268 role eliminations.
